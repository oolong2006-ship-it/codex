# AI-native opportunities for Saudi food & retail MSMEs (restaurants, cafes, cloud kitchens, bakeries, baqalas, Salla/Zid stores)

Research note scope: ~20 tool calls on 2026-10-31. Several primary sites (argaam.com, redseer.com, annahar.com, help.foodics.com) could not be fetched from this session (network allowlist); figures from them come from search-result summaries and are marked as such. Overall: market-size numbers are solid, loss numbers specific to Saudi SMEs are weak. Most "loss" figures are national aggregates, vendor claims, or non-Saudi benchmarks.

## Where do these businesses lose the most money? (and the 4-6 candidate problem areas)

### Takeaway
The best-documented money drains for Saudi F&B SMEs are (1) delivery-app economics (15-30% commissions, plus app prices 20-80% above in-store menus that hurt competitiveness), (2) food waste and over-ordering (KSA wastes about 33% of food, SAR 40bn/yr nationally), and (3) margin squeeze in a fast-crowding market (restaurant and cafe licences up 12.2% in 2025 while restaurant spending grew only about 1.6%). For retail and e-commerce: shrinkage, slow-moving or expiring stock, and COD rejections/returns. Saudi-specific evidence for these is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings

**Market scale**
- About 1.7M active SME commercial registrations at end of Q2/Q3 2025 (Monsha'at); about 8.88M SME employees at end of 2025. Regional split: Riyadh 39%, Makkah 17%, Eastern 16% (Qoyod SME report 2026). — [Search summary citing Monsha'at/Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/qoyod-sme-report-2026-final.pdf); [Ajel](https://ajel.sa/VQdMsf)
- Restaurant and cafe licences grew 12.2% in 2025, from 32,852 to 36,852, against 3.9% growth in all new commercial licences. Restaurant spending grew only about 1.6% in 2025 (the headline says spending rose 7.5% but "competition eats the margin"). Secondary source, not fetched; verify. — [Annahar (Arabic)](https://www.annahar.com/economy/352044/دراسة-إنفاق-السعوديين-على-المطاعم-يرتفع-75-لكن-المنافسة-تلتهم-الهامش)
- 7,322 new restaurant and cafe commercial records were issued in 2021 alone. — [Foodex Saudi](https://foodexsaudiexpo.com/en/news/579)
- More than 1,500 franchise brands operate in KSA, with F&B a leading sector (Monsha'at). — [Monsha'at](https://monshaat.gov.sa/en/node/491795)
- Foodics (the dominant KSA restaurant POS) supported 33,000+ restaurants by 2024 and processed $12bn GMV in 2025 (+20% YoY). — [Multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/foodics); [Forbes ME](https://www.forbesmiddleeast.com/lists/the-middle-easts-fintech-50-2026/foodics/)
- HungerStation reportedly has about 55,000 partner restaurants and stores in 102 cities (vendor blog figure). — [deonde](https://deonde.co/blog/hungerstation-business-model/)
- Salla said it had served 60,000+ online stores (cumulative, Feb 2025). — [Mubasher](https://sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21468634/alkhwlani-salla-supports-over-60000-online-stores?lang=en)
- Convenience-store segment: about 31,600 players (Ken Research, unverified). Baqalas must be at least 24 m² under the 2025 municipal rules, which also ban them from selling tobacco, dates, meat, fruit and vegetables. — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-convenience-store-retail-market.md); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabia-grocery-stores-banned-from-selling-tobacco-meat-fruit-and-vegetables-1.500175565)

**Candidate 1: Delivery-app profitability (multi-app pricing, commission and promo optimisation)**
- Commissions: Jahez typically 15-25% (big chains about 10%); HungerStation 20-30% (vendor blogs). One podcast summary puts the Jahez average at about 12%. No primary source publishes the rates. — [Menuviel](https://blog.menuviel.com/jahez-fees-and-commissions-for-restaurants/); [Miracuves](https://miracuves.com/blog/revenue-model-of-hungerstation/); [podcast summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/l42RJD1xAf0)
- A GAC study (Jan 2026) and Argaam monitoring found delivery-app prices 20% to more than 80% above restaurants' own prices on some meals, mostly 20-30%. Some apps had exclusive deals 20-30% cheaper than rival apps. HungerStation had the highest average delivery fee and Ninja the lowest. GAC issued fair-pricing guidelines in March 2025. — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1875921) (search summary; not fetched)
- Kuwait has fixed fees and commissions and banned exclusivity, so a Gulf regulatory trend exists. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/kuwait/kuwait-tightens-rules-on-food-delivery-apps-fixes-feesbans-exclusivity-1.500430205); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/analysis/food-drink/2026/02/gulf-watchdogs-get-tough-with-food-delivery-companies/)
- Redseer's 2026 KSA food delivery report is titled "Free delivery isn't free". — [Redseer](https://redseer.com/reports/ksa-food-delivery-free-delivery-isnt-free/) (not fetched)
- Digital channels add layers of cost (logistics, packaging, commission). Opinion only. — [Annahar summary](https://www.annahar.com/economy/352044/)

**Candidate 2: Waste and production/prep forecasting for restaurants, bakeries and cloud kitchens**
- KSA wastes more than 33% of food, about SAR 40bn/yr (Minister, 2021). An earlier ministry figure was SAR 49bn (2018). Restaurants and buffets are cited as a major share but not costed separately. — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1490504); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/node/1654461/amp)
- A 2020 decree requires restaurants and wedding halls to contract with food banks for surplus. — [Gulf Business](https://gulfbusiness.com/?p=126137)
- Winnow Vision (camera over the bin) is deployed in the UAE with about 90% accuracy; Winnow is based in Dubai. No confirmed Saudi deployments of Winnow, Leanpath or Orbisk were found. — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/uae%2Fenvironment%2Fuae-commercial-kitchens-use-artificial-intelligence-to-track-food-waste-1.66165239); [Khaleej Times](https://khaleejtimes.com/lifestyle/food/winnow-a-food-tech-startup-that-saves-36-million-meals-annually)

**Candidate 3: Food-cost / supplier-price control (invoice to recipe cost to menu price)**
- Supy's ingredient index showed KSA "Rising", with the restaurant basket up 3.3% vs July 2025. — [Supy weather report](https://supy.io/weather-report)
- Local frozen chicken once rose 36% in a single month (GASTAT, undated). KSA imports about 80% of its food (2022). — [Madhyamam](https://english.madhyamam.com/mefriend/gnrl/chicken-and-potato-tops-in-price-rise-list-1086272); [AGBI](https://www.agbi.com/?p=84509)
- Headline CPI was mild (2.2% in May 2025), so the pressure shows up as item volatility rather than general inflation. — [Trading Economics](https://jp.tradingeconomics.com/saudi-arabia/inflation-cpi/news/463027)

**Candidate 4: Shrinkage and cash/void fraud detection from POS logs (and optionally existing CCTV)**
- Claimed KSA shrink benchmark of 1-2% of sales, with above 2.5% signalling control problems (Qoyod glossary; unsourced). — [Qoyod](https://www.qoyod.com/en/glossary/inventory-shrinkage/)
- Globally, employee theft is about half of retail shrink, and cashier-related tactics (voids, fake receipts) are about 61% of employee shrink (general claim). — [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/2907/retail-shrinkage)
- In the US, 34% of SMB retailers saw shrink rise in the past year. — [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/resources/retail-shrink/)

**Candidate 5: Inventory, expiry and replenishment for baqalas and mini-markets**
- Rewaa (Saudi retail POS/inventory) serves 7,000+ retailers. It raised a $45M Series B in Dec 2025 specifically to make AI "core to the daily merchant experience". — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2025/12/saudi-arabias-rewaa-closes-45-million-series-b); [Mubasher](https://en.sharikatmubasher.com/media-hub/news/21526010/rewaa-closes-45m-series-b-to-expand-ai-powered-retail-solutions?lang=en)
- Small grocers "will have trouble keeping up" with modern retail (Gulf News analysis). — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/analysis/saudi-arabias-small-grocery-stores-will-have-trouble-keeping-up-1.63466208)

**Candidate 6: COD rejection / return-to-origin (RTO) risk for Salla/Zid stores**
- Historic claim: Saudi e-commerce return rates of almost 60% for some major brands (2015, opinion piece). Regional COD preference fell from 41% (2020) to 20% (2024); only about 10% of KSA/UAE/Kuwait shoppers choose cash (Checkout.com via Enterprise). — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/en/2015/10/cash-is-not-king-opinion); [Enterprise](https://enterpriseam.com/ksa/2024/05/15/online-shopping-in-saudi-has-seen-staggering-growth-since-pandemic/)
- Amazon sellers in KSA complain of significant losses from fake COD orders. — [Amazon seller forum](https://sellercentral-europe.amazon.com/seller-forums/discussions/t/fd268cf8-0812-45ba-8c85-8f5ae5ac11c3)

### Inferences
- **Who pays and at what price** (inference; no Saudi SME willingness-to-pay data found):
  - Foodics plans were historically about AED 199-417/month (Software Advice, 2022, probably outdated) ([source](https://www.softwareadvice.com/retail/foodics-profile/)).
  - Global menu-engineering SaaS (MarketMan, Craftable, Avero) costs $500-1,500/month (vendor claim) ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/ai-menu-engineering)). That is too expensive for a single-branch Saudi cafe.
  - A plausible volume price is about SAR 150-400/month per branch for owner-operators, or a performance fee (a share of commissions saved or waste avoided). The owner pays; at 2-10 branch chains, the ops manager or finance lead pays.
- **Candidate 1 is the most acute pain with the weakest tooling.** A restaurant on 3-4 apps (HungerStation, Jahez, Keeta, The Chefz, Ninja, ToYou) that sets markups, promos and "exclusive" deals per app needs a contribution-margin-per-order view by app and item, after commission, promo co-funding, packaging and refunds. An AI agent could:
  - reconcile app settlement statements (PDF/CSV) against POS orders;
  - flag commission or penalty leakage;
  - simulate markup and promo changes per app with price-elasticity models;
  - recommend which items to list, delist or bundle per app.
  - It uses only data the restaurant already has (settlement reports, POS export).
  - GAC scrutiny of price gaps is a tailwind that also creates risk: markups may become constrained.
- **Candidate 2: phone-camera, low-hardware waste logging is the innovative angle.** Staff photograph the waste tray or end-of-day leftovers, vision estimates the item and weight, and the result feeds a next-day prep/bake forecast using POS history, Hijri calendar effects (Ramadan, Eid, paydays on the 27th), weather and school calendars. Winnow and Leanpath target hotels and large kitchens with dedicated scales and cameras. Small bakeries and cloud kitchens are unserved. The Ramadan and payday seasonality is a genuine local angle global tools miss.
- **Candidate 3:** Arabic/English supplier invoices (often handwritten or semi-structured) can be parsed by multimodal LLMs into item prices. The tool then updates recipe costs and alerts the owner: "chicken cost +12% → shawarma margin now 58%, reprice or change portion". Supy covers part of this for mid-size chains. Micro operators run on paper and WhatsApp photos of invoices. Note: invoice capture is a commodity feature; the value is the price-to-recipe-to-menu loop.
- **Candidate 4:** Anomaly detection on POS void/refund/discount logs per cashier and shift is pure software on existing data. Adding vision on existing CCTV (no new hardware) is more novel but raises privacy and labour sensitivities.
- **Candidate 6:** A COD risk score (per order: phone history, address quality, basket, time) plus automated pre-dispatch confirmation could cut RTO costs. However, the COD share is falling, which shrinks this opportunity over time.

### Gaps
- No Saudi-specific data was found on restaurant food-cost %, net margins, closure or failure rates, or waste share in restaurants. GASTAT structural business statistics for accommodation and food service, and for wholesale and retail 2024, exist on stats.gov.sa but were not retrieved.
- No official commission rates from HungerStation, Jahez or Keeta. The GAC study primary document was not retrieved.
- No current active-merchant counts for Zid, and no active (rather than cumulative) count for Salla.
- No current Saudi COD rejection rate from a carrier or SPL.
- No Saudi data on baqala counts (official), shrink, or expiry write-offs.
- No Saudi SME willingness-to-pay survey for software.

## Which problems have no credible AI product in KSA, or competitors missing Arabic/local angles?

### Takeaway
Inventory forecasting and BI for restaurants are already contested in KSA: Foodics AI/BI has inventory and sales forecast dashboards, Supy runs AI inventory and procurement through the Foodics marketplace, and Rewaa just raised $45M for AI retail ops. Comparatively open areas:
- per-app delivery profitability optimisation;
- phone-camera waste and prep forecasting for small kitchens and bakeries;
- POS-log fraud and shrink detection for small chains;
- COD risk for Salla/Zid merchants.

### Cited Findings
- **Foodics AI / Foodics BI:** AI business intelligence (mobile and web) with Inventory Forecast and Sales Forecast dashboards, positioned on reducing waste. A $10M AWS deal funds a roadmap: predictive demand forecasting, intelligent inventory, dynamic pricing, AI recommendations. These are roadmap items, not confirmed launches. — [Foodics help (search summary)](https://help.foodics.com/hc/en-us/articles/22114135398684-What-are-Forecast-Dashboards-in-Foodics-AI); [Entrepreneur ME](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/growth-strategies/foodics-and-aws-sign-us10-million-agreement-to-power/498442); [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=34729)
- **Supy** (UAE, $8M seed in 2022 led by BECO): AI inventory, procurement and BI for restaurants. Available on the Foodics marketplace (Inventory Suppliers category) for KSA and other GCC countries, with a Riyadh office. Reported in 2026 as expanding to 42 countries. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/index.php/2022/07/supy-raises-8-million-seed-round); [Foodics portfolio](https://www.foodics.com/portfolio/supy); [Supy-Foodics integration](https://supy.io/integrations/foodics)
- **Tenzo** (UK, restaurant BI and forecasting) integrates with Supy. A direct Foodics integration is unconfirmed. — [Tenzo](https://www.gotenzo.com/integration/supy/)
- **Foodics marketplace** also lists ShopBrain and Grubtech, plus apps such as Sanad, Menuhat, Fawa and Locate. — [Foodics ShopBrain](https://www.foodics.com/portfolio/shopbrain-2); [Foodics Grubtech](https://www.foodics.com/portfolio/grubtech/)
- **Grubtech** (Dubai) and **Deliverect** (Belgium) aggregate delivery apps into one dashboard with reporting. Deliverect scores 2.5/5 on Capterra ZA from a small sample. Neither is positioned as a profit optimiser. — [Grubtech G2](https://www.g2.com/products/grubtech/discuss); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.za/software/197514/delivery-management-sofware)
- **NUMU NIA** (UAE): an AI agent for restaurants on delivery platforms, claiming up to 140% delivery revenue growth. This is the closest competitor to Candidate 1. — [Arab Founders](https://arabfounders.net/en/numu-nia-ai-agent-restaurant-delivery-growth-uae-gcc/)
- **SUFRA AI** (Qatar): AI menus, $100K pre-seed from Snoonu. — [MENA Startup Digest](https://menastartupdigest.com/?p=36402)
- **Food-waste vision:** Winnow (in the UAE; reported in 2026 to have acquired Lumitics, secondary source), Leanpath, Orbisk, KITRO. None was found deployed in KSA SMEs. — [simplify.jobs](https://simplify.jobs/c/Winnow); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/winnow/alternatives-competitors)
- **Rewaa** (Saudi; 7,000+ retailers; $45M Series B, Dec 2025) for AI retail ops. It is the main threat in baqala and retail inventory. — [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2025/12/saudi-arabias-rewaa-closes-45-million-series-b)
- **Salla** uses AI in Salla Ads and publishes an "AI for Commerce" developer guide, which means an app-store distribution channel exists. Zid's CTO said "the next phase is all about leveraging AI", but no specific product was found. — [Salla docs](https://docs.salla.dev/playbook/building/ai-commerce); [Entrepreneur ME](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/?p=818)
- **Global menu-engineering and inventory tools:** MarketMan, Craftable, Avero at $500-1,500/month (vendor claim). — [RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/ai-menu-engineering)

### Inferences
- **Avoid a generic "restaurant inventory + forecasting" product.** Foodics owns the POS data and is building this natively, and Supy sits in its marketplace. Any F&B product should ship as a Foodics marketplace app (distribution) and do what Foodics is unlikely to prioritise. The other POS systems in KSA (e.g., Marn, Loyverse, Odoo-based ones) were not researched.
- **The open niches are each a layer between the restaurant and someone else's platform:**
  - between the restaurant and the delivery apps (settlement reconciliation, per-app pricing);
  - between the restaurant and its suppliers (price-to-recipe loop for micro operators who are not on Supy);
  - between the restaurant and its staff (POS fraud).
- **The local angles global tools lack:**
  - Arabic handwritten invoices;
  - Saudi delivery-app settlement formats;
  - Hijri seasonality;
  - bilingual staff (Arabic, Urdu, Bengali, Tagalog), relevant for photo-logging UIs used by kitchen workers.
- **The biggest risk is NUMU NIA** in delivery optimisation (if it enters KSA). Platform-side tools (HungerStation and Jahez merchant analytics) could also commoditise basic insights.
- **Baqalas:** Rewaa, plus Foodics-like retail POS players, make baqala inventory crowded at the top. Most micro baqalas may not use any POS. The 2025 product bans (no tobacco, produce or meat) reshape their assortment, which is an opportunity for assortment and replenishment advice, but the willingness to pay of micro baqalas is doubtful.

### Gaps
- Foodics AI feature details and pricing could not be fetched (help.foodics.com blocked), and the full Foodics marketplace app list was not retrieved.
- Did not verify whether NUMU, Winnow or Tenzo have Saudi customers.
- Did not research Saudi POS alternatives (Marn, Odoo partners, Loyverse share), Salla/Zid app-store apps for COD risk (none surfaced), or Keeta's (Meituan) commission policy in KSA.
- No Saudi competitors were found for POS-log fraud detection. Absence of evidence is not proof of absence.

## Which problems need only data the business already has (POS exports, photos, invoices), with no government APIs, WhatsApp bots or heavy hardware?

### Takeaway
All six candidates can run on business-owned data:
- delivery settlement CSV/PDFs plus a POS export (Candidate 1);
- phone photos plus POS sales (2);
- photographed supplier invoices plus recipes (3);
- POS void/refund logs (4);
- POS or Rewaa stock data, or shelf photos (5);
- Salla/Zid order history through the platform app APIs, which are private-platform APIs, not government ones (6).

Candidates 1, 3 and 4 have the cleanest data path. Candidate 2 needs a staff habit (photo logging). Candidates 5 and 6 depend on POS or platform-app adoption.

### Cited Findings
- The Foodics marketplace offers authorised install, branch and item mapping, and real-time POS sales sync for third-party apps (shown by Supy's integration), so a POS-data path exists without hardware. — [Foodics help/Supy](https://foodics.helpjuice.com/1669765-supy); [Foodics portfolio](https://www.foodics.com/portfolio/supy)
- Salla documents building AI apps for its merchants, so a merchant-data path exists for e-commerce. — [Salla docs](https://docs.salla.dev/playbook/building/ai-commerce)
- Winnow and Leanpath depend on dedicated camera-plus-scale hardware, the hardware dependency a phone-based approach avoids. — [CB Insights / Leanpath summary](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/leanpath/alternatives-competitors); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2022/12/07/food-for-thought-how-ai-is-reducing-waste-in-restaurants)
- The SFDA requirement for calorie and nutrition info on menus from 1 July 2025 is noted only as context. Compliance products are excluded from scope. — [search summary](https://www.foodics.com/?p=76160)

### Inferences
- **Ranked by data-readiness × pain × competitive openness** (judgement, not measured):
  1. Delivery-app profit optimiser and settlement reconciler: high pain, data in hand, NUMU the only near competitor.
  2. Invoice-to-recipe cost and price-alert loop for micro F&B: data in hand via photos; Supy is a partial competitor upmarket.
  3. Phone-camera waste plus prep/bake forecasting for bakeries and cloud kitchens: novel; needs a habit; Foodics forecasting overlaps on the forecast part.
  4. POS void/refund fraud detection for 2-20 branch chains: data in hand; sensitive; little visible competition.
  5. COD/RTO risk scoring for Salla/Zid: data via platform apps; the market is shrinking as COD declines.
  6. Baqala replenishment and expiry: crowded (Rewaa), with low willingness to pay and low POS penetration at the micro end.
- **Obstacles across all candidates:**
  - Platform dependence: Foodics could clone a feature or restrict its marketplace, and delivery apps could change settlement formats or forbid scraping. Use merchant-uploaded files rather than scraping.
  - SME churn in a crowded market, since licences grow much faster than spending.
  - Low willingness to pay among micro operators.
  - Proving ROI fast enough.
  - Privacy and labour sensitivity in fraud detection.
  - Staff turnover undermines photo-logging habits.

### Gaps
- No primary evidence on how many Saudi SMEs on Foodics or Salla actually export or upload data, or on the API rate limits and terms for third-party apps on Foodics, Salla and Zid.
- No field data on whether Saudi micro F&B owners keep digital supplier invoices.
